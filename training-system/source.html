How often to weigh — it changes by phase Now, during the cut: once a week is fine. You have a stopping rule and photos doing the real work, so weight is only there to tell you the rule has been met. One reading a week is enough for that.

During the reverse: once a week is too coarse. You will be deciding whether to add 75 kcal every 4–5 days — which means a single weigh-in per decision, and a single reading carries the full 2–4 lb of daily noise from water, glycogen and gut contents. Two weeks could differ by 3 lb for no reason at all and you would step the calories on it. Go to three times a week, or daily with the number hidden, so there is an average underneath the decision.

Whichever you pick: same days, same time, after the toilet, before food or drink. And never act on one reading — the average or nothing.

Your Health — The Vital Bits

The standard coeliac test cannot work on you tTG-IgA is an IgA-based test and you cannot make enough IgA to trigger it, so it reads negative either way. Ask for tTG-IgG and DGP-IgG, and say "I have selective IgA deficiency" when you ask. It is a known pitfall that still gets missed.
Get bloods done — three symptoms, one likely cause Mouth ulcers, flat taste and intermittent tiredness all point at the same short list: iron, B12, folate, zinc. Flat taste is a textbook zinc sign. Ulcers are a textbook iron/B12/folate sign — though IgA deficiency causes them directly too.

Ask for: ferritin, FBC, B12, folate, vitamin D, zinc, tTG-IgG, DGP-IgG, thyroid. Zinc is not standard — ask by name and mention the ulcers and taste. Ask for the ferritin number: 20 gets called normal and will still flatten you.
Ask your immunologist about raw dairy while you are there IgA deficiency varies enormously — many people with it are entirely asymptomatic. Which you are depends on your infection history and other immunoglobulin levels, which they know and I do not. If you are the common type they will likely say it is fine.
Raw cheese, done properly Aged hard, 60 days plus — Comté, Gruyère, aged cheddar, parmesan, Manchego. Skip raw soft and blue — higher moisture and pH, and that is the category that actually causes problems. Buy a piece, cut it yourself — contamination happens at handling, not inside the wheel. Below 4°C — listeria grows at fridge temperature.
Oats and salt Drained only sometimes on GF oats points at contamination hot spots — most are machine-sorted, so stray grains come in clumps. Look for purity protocol oats, or leave them out; rice and potato do the same job.

On salt: stopping over-salting was right. Do not go below 2,300–3,000mg — you run four times a week in heat and drink 3–3.5L, and restricting sodium there drops plasma volume. March's 5 lb was water, not fat.
Illness costs you more than it costs a training partner Hard exercise lowers mucosal IgA in everyone; you start lower and a 29% deficit lowers it further. Do not train through illness, take the deload weeks, protect sleep, reverse on schedule. Two weeks ill costs more than any session gains.

PB Week — Week 13

Three attempts in seven days. The training is already banked by then; this week is about arriving fresh and not throwing one away in the first 200m.

The weekSunday 5K · Wednesday mile · Saturday 400m. Two clear easy or rest days before each. Never move an attempt closer to the one before it — the 400m is last because it wants the freshest legs, and it is the one where fatigue costs you most.
Eating, 2–3h beforeFamiliar carbs, low fat, low fibre — porridge, toast and honey, rice. Nothing you have not eaten before a session. Stop drinking large volumes 45 min out, sip after that.
CaffeineIf you use it, 45–60 min before, and only at a dose you have already trained on. PB day is not the day to try it for the first time.
Warm-up — start 45 min before the gun15 min easy jog · drills · 3–4 build-ups finishing at target pace · 5–8 min rest, staying warm · go. Same warm-up you have done all block, no improvising.
Between attemptsDay after: 20 min easy jog to clear it, or walk. Day before: full rest. Keep the calories up all week — a deficit on PB week costs you the times you have trained for.
How to run each one
5K — 16:50 is 3:22/kmEven splits, and the first kilometre is the one that ruins it. Going through in 3:15 feels effortless and costs you 20 seconds over the last two kilometres. If anything, run the first km 2–3s slow.
Mile — 4:50 is 72s lapsFirst lap no faster than 70. Lap 3 is where the race is won: that is the one that drifts to 75 without you noticing, so it is the one to actively push.
400m — 55 is roughly 26.5 / 28.5Committed from the gun, but relaxed — the last 100m is decided by how little you tie up, not by how hard you drive. Loose jaw, loose shoulders. Fighting the tightness makes it worse.

Training in a Heat Wave

Heat changes what the scale says, what your paces mean and what your sodium needs are — all at once, and all temporarily.

Your weight will read high, and it isn't fatHeat acclimation expands plasma volume by 10–20% within about 5–10 days — that is a genuine adaptation, and it is literally extra fluid. Add the aldosterone-driven sodium retention that comes with heavy sweating and you can carry 1–3 lb that has nothing to do with body composition. It masks fat loss; it does not stop it. Do not cut calories in response.
Your paces will be slower — that is correct, not a failureBlood is diverted to the skin to shed heat, so less goes to working muscle. The same effort buys you a slower pace. Run the sessions on effort, not the target number, and expect 5–15s per km slower in real heat. Logging a 4/5 on an "easy" session in 30°C is the heat, not lost fitness.
Sodium goes up, not just waterDrinking a lot of water while under-replacing salt is how you end up flat, headachy and cramping. Aim at the top of the 2,300–3,000mg sodium range or above it on heavy sweat days, and keep potassium up with it. Weigh yourself before and after a long session if you want the real number — each pound lost is roughly 450ml to replace.
Sleep takes the hit, and everything followsCore temperature has to fall to enter deep sleep, which a hot bedroom prevents. Poor sleep raises cortisol, cortisol holds fluid, and now the scale is lying to you for a second reason. Cool the room hard — this is the week that habit earns its place.
The upsideHeat training is a genuine stimulus. That plasma volume expansion improves cardiac output, and it carries over to cooler conditions for a few weeks afterwards. Ride it out and you come out of the heat wave fitter than you went in.

The Honest Anti-Ageing Ranking

Your diet is near the ceiling of what diet can do — and that ceiling is lower than you think You are at about 8 out of 10 and the remaining two points are worth very little in absolute terms. There is no version of a food list that is "the best anti-ageing thing going," because diet is roughly fifth on the list of things that actually change how fast you age. Chasing the last 20% of a food plan while anything above it is unattended is the most common way people spend a decade optimising the wrong variable.
What actually moves biological ageing, in order
1 · Cardiorespiratory fitnessThe strongest modifiable predictor of all-cause mortality in large cohorts, and the effect size dwarfs any dietary intervention ever measured. You already do this. Your easy runs are not separate from the ageing goal — they are the main event.
2 · Muscle mass and strengthAmong the best predictors of healthy ageing, and the thing a long deficit costs you first. Four gym sessions and 155–160g of protein are doing more here than any food on the nutrition page.
3 · Sleep7–9 hours. DNA repair, growth hormone, glymphatic clearance, cortisol, appetite, and how your face looks in the morning. Nothing substitutes for it and nothing compensates for missing it.
4 · Not accumulating damageNo smoking, minimal alcohol, sun protection, avoiding injury, and not sitting in a deep deficit for months. Mostly things you don't do.
5 · DietReal, worth doing, and this far down the list. You have already captured most of what is available here.
For how you actually look, one thing beats the entire food page combined Somewhere around 80% of visible facial ageing is photoageing — UV damage, not intrinsic ageing. Wrinkles, laxity, uneven pigment, the texture change. In a controlled trial, people using sunscreen daily showed no detectable increase in skin ageing over four and a half years while the control group did.

No food produces an effect anywhere near that. Your SPF50 is already in the routine and it is quietly the single highest-value item in this entire app. Daily, all year, not just when it is sunny.
Tretinoin — withdrawn, your skin does not tolerate retinoids Leaving this here so the reasoning stays visible rather than quietly disappearing: tretinoin has the strongest topical evidence for reversing photoageing, but it is a stronger retinoid than the retinol already causing you puffiness and irritation. It is not an option. See the Skincare tab for bakuchiol, which is.
The biggest upgrade genuinely available to you: tretinoin over retinol Prescription tretinoin has the strongest evidence of any topical for reversing photoageing — measurable collagen synthesis on biopsy, not just self-reported improvement. Over-the-counter retinol is a weaker precursor your skin has to convert, at a fraction of the potency.

If you are serious about the appearance side, ask a GP or dermatologist about tretinoin. It is cheap, it is decades old, and it will do more than every polyphenol on the nutrition page put together. Same rules as your retinol: start twice weekly, buffer with the balm, and non-negotiable sunscreen.
Is your routine chronic stress? Partly, yes Not the training — that is acute stress with recovery built in, which is the useful kind. Three things here are genuinely chronic.

1 · The deficit. A 29% energy deficit held for weeks is a continuous physiological stressor. Cortisol up, testosterone down, recovery slower, water retained. This is the biggest one by a distance, and it has a fixed answer: get to the band and reverse on schedule. It ends when you end it.

2 · Load stacking. Three hard sessions, four gym sessions, a deficit, studying and a heat wave, all at once. Each is survivable; together they compound. The deload weeks exist for exactly this and they are the first thing people skip.

3 · The tracking itself. The awkward one, and you asked for no sugarcoating. You weigh every gram and log weight, HR, energy, sessions, feel ratings, niggles and a weekly food list — and I built the app that made that easier. Past a point, measuring the thing becomes its own stressor. The line is not how much you track but whether it feels calm or anxious: whether a bad scale day ruins the morning, whether you would skip a meal out because you could not weigh it, whether it runs in the background of your head all day. If any of that is true, the tracking has stopped serving you.
What actually helps — including one you are already doing You went out with a mate two weeks running. That is not a distraction from the plan — it is one of the highest-value things on this whole page, and it matters more than your grape intake does. Keep doing it.

Only track what changes a decision. The test for every metric: has this number ever made you do something differently? Weight trend and morning HR pass. Session feel passes. If the weekly food checklist has never once altered a meal, drop it — a metric that changes nothing is pure cognitive load.

One untracked day a week. Eat without weighing, log nothing. If that idea feels genuinely uncomfortable, that discomfort is the answer to your question.

Keep the easy runs easy. Zone 2 lowers stress, hard sessions add it, and the ratio in your week decides whether training is a net regulator or a net stressor. Nasal breathing enforces it.

Then the ordinary things: sleep, morning daylight, magnesium, the ten minutes of meditation you already do, and taking the deload weeks when they arrive.
The one nobody wants to hear Social connection and chronic psychological stress carry effect sizes on mortality comparable to smoking — larger than obesity, and far larger than anything on your plate. It appears in almost no "anti-ageing protocol" because it cannot be bought or optimised. If you are studying hard, training hard, tracking everything and eating alone, that is a real ageing input and it will not show up in any of the numbers this app tracks. On your own evidence you are not doing that, which is worth more than it sounds.
Don't waste money on these Resveratrol — the foundational results failed to replicate and oral bioavailability is poor. NMN and NR — they raise NAD+ markers, but human outcome data remains unimpressive. Metformin in a healthy young athlete — no longevity evidence in non-diabetics, and reasonable evidence it blunts adaptation to endurance training, which for you is actively counterproductive. Rapamycin — no human longevity data, real immunosuppression risk. Epigenetic age tests — too noisy at the individual level to track anything you are doing.

The only supplement here with genuinely strong evidence is creatine, and it is already on your stage 3 list.
And the reframe that matters most at your age You are in your twenties. There is not much ageing to slow yet — the returns available to you are almost entirely in not accumulating damage (sun, alcohol, chronic stress, injury, prolonged under-eating) and building reserve (muscle, VO2max, bone density) that you spend in forty years' time. The bone density you build now, and the cardiac and mitochondrial capacity from this block, are the actual anti-ageing intervention. The food supports it. It is not the thing itself.

Which is genuinely good news: you are already doing the top three. The diet is close to done. Wear sunscreen, sleep, ask about tretinoin, don't isolate yourself, and stop looking for a food that does what training and sleep already do.

Retinoids are out — so ignore what I said about tretinoin Puffiness plus irritation even going slowly means your skin is not tolerating it, and prescription tretinoin is stronger, not gentler. Pushing through would make it worse. Scratch that recommendation entirely.

One thing worth checking first though: it may be the vehicle rather than the retinol. Many retinol products carry alcohol, fragrance or particular emulsifiers, and irritant reactions often track the formula rather than the active. If you ever want to retest, do it with an encapsulated retinol in a bland base, on a patch behind the ear for two weeks. If that also puffs up, it is genuinely the retinoid and you are done with the class.
Bakuchiol is the real substitute, and the evidence is better than people assume A head-to-head trial put 0.5% bakuchiol against 0.5% retinol and found comparable improvement in wrinkles and pigmentation with significantly less irritation and scaling. It is not a marketing dupe — it is the one alternative with an actual comparison study behind it. Nightly, build up from 2–3 nights a week.
Dropping retinoids gives you two ingredients back You removed vitamin C and niacinamide to keep the total irritation load down while on retinol. That constraint is now gone.

Vitamin C in the morning — the collagen cofactor, works with your SPF rather than against it, and pairs with the collagen peptides you already take. Niacinamide at night — barrier function and redness, and it is one of the best-tolerated actives there is, which matters for skin like yours. Add one at a time, a fortnight apart, so you know which is which if something flares.
