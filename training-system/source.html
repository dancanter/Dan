Which weeks run reduced volume. Default is 4, 8 and 12 — every fourth week. Move them to line up with time off, or set 0 to remove them entirely.

Shows only the 7-day average and hides individual daily numbers. Nothing is deleted — the daily figure is mostly water and has never once been the number you act on.

How often to weigh — it changes by phase Now, during the cut: once a week is fine. You have a stopping rule and photos doing the real work, so weight is only there to tell you the rule has been met. One reading a week is enough for that.

During the reverse: once a week is too coarse. You will be deciding whether to add 75 kcal every 4–5 days — which means a single weigh-in per decision, and a single reading carries the full 2–4 lb of daily noise from water, glycogen and gut contents. Two weeks could differ by 3 lb for no reason at all and you would step the calories on it. Go to three times a week, or daily with the number hidden, so there is an average underneath the decision.

Whichever you pick: same days, same time, after the toilet, before food or drink. And never act on one reading — the average or nothing.

The evening bowl never grows — it is your fattiest meal, so calories added there go to fat rather than glycogen.

Around step 2 or 3 the scale jumps ~2 lb. Ignore it.More carbs means more glycogen, and glycogen holds three times its weight in water. It lands within a week then settles. A pound of fat takes 3,500 surplus calories; you are adding 75 a day.
Fats come after, not duringButter, aged cheese, fattier cuts — all fine, once maintenance is confirmed and held 2–3 weeks. Carbs restore metabolic rate; fat barely does. Add fat during the climb and you spend your calorie ceiling before the rate recovers. Watch protein when the cuts get fattier — 200g chicken breast is 62g protein, 200g ribeye about 46g for nearly double the calories.
