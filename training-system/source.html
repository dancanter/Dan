Cellular Ageing — The Levers That Move

The tension you have to periodise, not solve Autophagy, AMPK and caloric restriction are the longevity levers — and every one of them suppresses muscle protein synthesis and recovery. mTOR activation, high protein and energy availability are the performance levers — and chronic mTOR activation is the opposite of the longevity signal. You cannot maximise both at once. Anyone selling you a protocol that does both simultaneously is selling you something. The resolution is to run them in phases, which is exactly what the stages above already do: the cut is your AMPK and autophagy window, maintenance is your mTOR and repair window. Trying to run both at once is how people get neither.

Ranked by what the evidence actually supports at your age. The order matters — the top three outweigh everything below them combined.

1 · VO2maxThe strongest single predictor of all-cause mortality in the epidemiology — stronger than smoking status, blood pressure or cholesterol. Your 5K work is not adjacent to the anti-ageing goal. It is the intervention.
2 · Zone 2 — the easy runDrives PGC-1α, which drives mitochondrial biogenesis: more mitochondria, better ones. The 45–70 min easy run is the most genuinely anti-ageing session in this entire block, and it is the one that feels least like training. Do not skip it because it feels too easy — feeling too easy is the point.
3 · Muscle massAmong the best predictors of healthy ageing there is, and the thing a long deficit costs you first. Four gym sessions and 155–160g of protein are doing more for this goal than any food on the list above.
4 · SleepDNA repair, the overnight growth hormone pulse, and glymphatic clearance of metabolic waste from the brain. Nothing on the food list substitutes for it, and nothing on the food list is damaged by it.
5 · Everything on the food pageReal, worth doing, and a fraction of the four above. Keep the ordering honest in your head — it is what stops you optimising pomegranate while sleeping six hours.
The one thing here that could genuinely age you A lean male endurance athlete sitting in a long deficit is heading toward relative energy deficiency — suppressed testosterone, falling bone density, impaired immune function, blunted recovery. That is a pro-ageing state, and it outweighs every polyphenol on this page put together. Which is the real reason the reverse is not optional and the timeline matters. Get to the band, confirm it, and start adding calories back.
Four levers most people miss
Antioxidant timing — the easy mistakeThe free radicals your training produces are the adaptation signal. Flooding the post-session window with concentrated antioxidants blunts it — the same mechanism as the cold plunge note in Protocols. Keep the loaded stuff (pomegranate, heavy berries, EVOO by the spoon) away from the two hours after a hard session. Eat them at other meals instead. Whole-food amounts at other times are fine.
Glycine → glutathioneYour collagen peptides are quietly feeding glutathione synthesis too — the body's master antioxidant, for which glycine is often the rate-limiting substrate. A genuine two-for-one, and a reason to keep the peptides going through every stage.
How you cook, not just whatHigh dry heat — searing, grilling, frying — generates advanced glycation end-products, which are one of the more direct dietary contributors to cellular ageing. Moist, lower heat — poaching, stewing, slow-cooking — generates a fraction of them. Your bone broth and stews are already on the right side. The practical change is small: stop charring the beef.
A 12–14h overnight fastLong enough to matter, short enough not to touch training quality or your protein total. Anything longer starts competing with a deficit and three hard sessions a week, and loses.

Two Honest Notes

Liver — once a week, genuinely100g of beef liver carries roughly five times the daily upper limit for vitamin A. Weekly is excellent; daily accumulates, and retinol toxicity shows up as headaches, joint pain and hair loss. The rest of this list has no ceiling worth worrying about. This one does.
Raw dairy — I'd use pasteurisedPasteurisation costs you almost nothing nutritionally: protein, calcium and most vitamins survive it, and the enzymes people cite as the reason for raw don't survive stomach acid either way. What it removes is listeria, salmonella, campylobacter and E. coli O157. You're training four times a week in a deficit with a PB week to reach — a week of campylobacter would cost you more than every marginal gain on this page combined. Kefir and yoghurt give you live cultures from pasteurised milk with none of that risk.

Reversing Without Putting It Back On

There is nothing magic in a reverse diet. It works because you are restoring metabolic rate and daily movement, both of which fall during a cut. Which means it only works if you enforce three things.

1 · Steps stay at 12,500–13,000. Non-negotiable.Non-exercise movement is the largest variable in your daily burn and the one that silently collapses during a cut — you fidget less, walk slower, take the lift. If steps drift down while calories go up, you regain, and it will feel like the reverse failed when actually the step count did. This single number does more than everything else on this page.
2 · Every added calorie is a carb.Protein stays 155–160g, fat stays 55–60g, fixed. Carbohydrate restores leptin and thyroid T3 fastest, which is the actual mechanism by which metabolic rate comes back — and it improves session quality, which raises expenditure again. Adding the calories as fat does far less of this.
3 · Keep the progressions running.Added calories partition toward glycogen and muscle when there is a training stimulus demanding them, and toward fat when there is not. The shrinking recoveries and rising rep counts in this block are not separate from the reverse — they are what makes it work.
The number that makes people quit — know it in advance Going from roughly 150g to 250g of carbs stores about 300g of extra glycogen, and glycogen holds around three times its own weight in water. That is roughly 2 lb on the scale in week two that contains no fat whatsoever. It arrives fast, then settles. Abandoning the reverse here is the single most common way this goes wrong, and it is entirely avoidable by expecting it.
Telling refill from real gain Refill is fast and then stops — up sharply for one to two weeks, then flat. Fat gain is slow and keeps going. So the test is simple: if the 7-day average is still climbing three weeks in, that is real, and you drop back one 75 kcal step. If it jumped and then went flat, that was water and you carry on stepping up.
Shredded and fast are not the same weight Worth saying plainly before you get to 122: the leanest you can get and the fastest you can run are usually a couple of pounds apart, and the fastest is the heavier one. Below roughly 10% body fat, recovery, sleep and testosterone all start degrading, and session quality goes with them. If you get to the band and the sessions are getting harder at unchanged paces, that is not a reason to cut further — check the Adaptation tab, and let the reverse do its job.

Salt Sensitivity — What's Actually Happening

It's water, and cutting salt is the wrong fix A gram of sodium holds roughly 100–200ml of water, so a salty day can put 1–3 lb on the scale overnight. It is gone in two or three days and it was never fat. The instinct is to cut sodium — but you are an endurance runner sweating hard in a heat wave, and under-replacing sodium drops your plasma volume, flattens your sessions, brings on cramp and headaches, and raises aldosterone. Higher aldosterone means you retain harder the next time you do eat salt. Chronic salt restriction makes salt sensitivity worse, not better.
The actual lever is potassium, not sodium Fluid balance runs off the sodium-to-potassium ratio, not sodium alone. Push potassium to the top of the 3,500–4,700mg range — avocado, potato with skin, banana, coconut water, leafy greens, salmon, yoghurt — and the same sodium intake retains substantially less water. This is the single most effective thing you can do about puffiness, and it works with your training rather than against it.
And keep it steady Erratic sodium is what produces the big swings you are noticing. A consistent intake day to day gives you a scale you can actually read; spiking and restricting gives you 3 lb of noise sitting on top of a 0.3 lb weekly signal.

"I Put Loads On When I Eat A Lot"

Check the arithmetic — it is physically not fat A pound of fat is 3,500 kcal above maintenance. To put on 3 lb of actual fat in a day you would need to eat about 12,000 calories on top of what you burn. What a big day actually adds is food still in your gut (1–2 lb), glycogen and the water bound to it (a large carb meal stores 2–3 lb of water), and sodium. All of it clears within a few days.
Why this one matters more than it looks This belief is the single most likely thing to make your reverse diet fail. The reverse works by adding calories back deliberately, and week two of it will put 2 lb on the scale from glycogen water — exactly the pattern you are describing. If you read that as fat and pull the calories back, you stay stuck at 1,800 kcal with a suppressed metabolic rate indefinitely. Knowing it is coming is what gets you through it.

Cortisol, Puffiness & Looking Lean

What you are describing — holding water, puffiness, a face that changes day to day — is mostly cortisol and fluid distribution, not body fat. These are the levers, in order.

Enough carbohydrate — yes, reallyTraining hard on low carb availability raises cortisol substantially. The 200–210g on hard days is not a concession, it is the cortisol control. Dropping carbs to accelerate the cut is the fastest way to a puffier face and a worse session in the same week.
MagnesiumLost heavily in sweat, and directly involved in regulating the stress axis and sleep depth. Pumpkin seeds, dark leafy greens, 85% dark chocolate, almonds, avocado. Deficiency is common in endurance athletes and it looks exactly like poor stress tolerance and broken sleep.
Omega-3 — 2–3× a weekOne of the few dietary interventions shown to blunt the cortisol response to stress, and it works on the inflammation side of puffiness at the same time.
Water, more than you thinkCounterintuitive but reliable: under-drinking raises ADH and you retain more. 3–3.5L, more in this heat. Chronic mild dehydration is a common cause of the puffiness people blame on salt.
Glycine before bedAbout 3g lowers core temperature and improves sleep quality in trials. Your collagen peptides already supply it — take them in the evening on days you are not training late and you get both effects from one thing.
Vitamin CYour adrenal glands hold the highest concentration of vitamin C in the body and stress depletes it. Citrus, peppers, berries — daily, and it doubles as the collagen cofactor.
Alcohol — the fastest route to a puffy faceOne night shows on your face for two days: dehydration, poor sleep architecture, inflammation, and a direct hit to the next day's session. Nothing else on this page moves the needle as fast in the wrong direction.
Sleep, againIt is the largest single input to facial puffiness there is, through cortisol and overnight fluid distribution. In a heat wave this is the habit to defend hardest — cool the room properly.
The honest summaryAdequate carbs, high potassium, steady sodium, plenty of water, real sleep, no alcohol. That combination does more for how lean and defined you look than any further calorie cut would — and unlike a calorie cut, none of it costs you your sessions.

PB Week — Week 13

Three attempts in seven days. The training is already banked by then; this week is about arriving fresh and not throwing one away in the first 200m.

The weekSunday 5K · Wednesday mile · Saturday 400m. Two clear easy or rest days before each. Never move an attempt closer to the one before it — the 400m is last because it wants the freshest legs, and it is the one where fatigue costs you most.
Eating, 2–3h beforeFamiliar carbs, low fat, low fibre — porridge, toast and honey, rice. Nothing you have not eaten before a session. Stop drinking large volumes 45 min out, sip after that.
CaffeineIf you use it, 45–60 min before, and only at a dose you have already trained on. PB day is not the day to try it for the first time.
Warm-up — start 45 min before the gun15 min easy jog · drills · 3–4 build-ups finishing at target pace · 5–8 min rest, staying warm · go. Same warm-up you have done all block, no improvising.
Between attemptsDay after: 20 min easy jog to clear it, or walk. Day before: full rest. Keep the calories up all week — a deficit on PB week costs you the times you have trained for.
How to run each one
5K — 16:50 is 3:22/kmEven splits, and the first kilometre is the one that ruins it. Going through in 3:15 feels effortless and costs you 20 seconds over the last two kilometres. If anything, run the first km 2–3s slow.
Mile — 4:50 is 72s lapsFirst lap no faster than 70. Lap 3 is where the race is won: that is the one that drifts to 75 without you noticing, so it is the one to actively push.
400m — 55 is roughly 26.5 / 28.5Committed from the gun, but relaxed — the last 100m is decided by how little you tie up, not by how hard you drive. Loose jaw, loose shoulders. Fighting the tightness makes it worse.

Log each attempt on the Progress tab as you finish it — the benchmark bars close against your block-start times.

Training in a Heat Wave

Heat changes what the scale says, what your paces mean and what your sodium needs are — all at once, and all temporarily.

Your weight will read high, and it isn't fatHeat acclimation expands plasma volume by 10–20% within about 5–10 days — that is a genuine adaptation, and it is literally extra fluid. Add the aldosterone-driven sodium retention that comes with heavy sweating and you can carry 1–3 lb that has nothing to do with body composition. It masks fat loss; it does not stop it. Do not cut calories in response.
Your paces will be slower — that is correct, not a failureBlood is diverted to the skin to shed heat, so less goes to working muscle. The same effort buys you a slower pace. Run the sessions on effort, not the target number, and expect 5–15s per km slower in real heat. Logging a 4/5 on an "easy" session in 30°C is the heat, not lost fitness.
Sodium goes up, not just waterDrinking a lot of water while under-replacing salt is how you end up flat, headachy and cramping. Aim at the top of the 2,300–3,000mg sodium range or above it on heavy sweat days, and keep potassium up with it. Weigh yourself before and after a long session if you want the real number — each pound lost is roughly 450ml to replace.
Sleep takes the hit, and everything followsCore temperature has to fall to enter deep sleep, which a hot bedroom prevents. Poor sleep raises cortisol, cortisol holds fluid, and now the scale is lying to you for a second reason. Cool the room hard — this is the week that habit earns its place.
The upsideHeat training is a genuine stimulus. That plasma volume expansion improves cardiac output, and it carries over to cooler conditions for a few weeks afterwards. Ride it out and you come out of the heat wave fitter than you went in.

When to Pull Back

Any two of these together means back off, not push harder. A week of backing off costs you almost nothing; pushing through costs you the block.

Target pace missed on rep 1 of a session — not rep 5, that's normal
Session-feel ratings climbing week to week at the same pace
Morning HR 5+ bpm above baseline for 3+ days running
Facial puffiness returning despite good sleep and sodium/potassium balance
Sleep quality or motivation dropping off
Soreness that doesn't clear within 48h

The response: drop max-speed work entirely for 2–3 weeks, space the Tuesday speed session to every 10 days, add a rest day. Keep calories where they are — never cut further as a response to fatigue. If it persists past two weeks, bring the deload week forward rather than waiting for the schedule.
