## 1. Urgent pathways

The highest-consequence content in the app: 13 entries, each reached
from a permanently visible Get help tab. Every one follows the same order — what to do now,
then why it matters, then reassurance only where it is true. Please check the **action line**
first; it is what someone frightened will read and act on.

### `movements` — My baby is moving less, or differently

**Routes to:** maternity-unit  ·  **Evidence type:** UK guidance

**ACTION LINE (shown first, largest):**

> Call your maternity unit now. Any hour, day or night.

**Explicit "do not" items:**

> - Don’t wait until morning.
> - Don’t try to make your baby move first — no cold drinks, no lying down, no sugary food.
> - Don’t use a home doppler. Hearing a heartbeat does not mean your baby is well, and it can falsely reassure you.

**Why it matters (shown after the action):**

> A change in your baby’s pattern can be the earliest sign that something needs checking. There is no target number of movements and no need to count kicks — what matters is that this feels different from your baby’s normal.

**Reassurance (shown last, only where true):**

> Most people who report one episode of reduced movements go on to have a straightforward pregnancy and a healthy baby. Checking early is exactly the right thing to do, and there is no limit on how many times you can go in.

### `bleeding` — I’m bleeding

**Routes to:** maternity-unit  ·  **Evidence type:** UK guidance

**ACTION LINE (shown first, largest):**

> Call your maternity unit now — however light the bleeding is.

**Explicit "do not" items:**

> - Use a pad rather than a tampon, so the bleeding can be assessed properly.

**Why it matters (shown after the action):**

> Bleeding has many causes in pregnancy, some completely harmless and some that need treating quickly. It is not something to judge for yourself at home.

**Reassurance (shown last, only where true):**

> Many pregnancies with early bleeding continue perfectly normally. Calling is the right response regardless of how it turns out.

### `severe-pain` — I have severe or sudden pain

**Routes to:** emergency  ·  **Evidence type:** UK guidance

**ACTION LINE (shown first, largest):**

> Call 999 if the pain is severe, constant, or you can’t focus through it — especially with bleeding, shoulder-tip pain, or feeling faint. Otherwise call your maternity unit now.

**Why it matters (shown after the action):**

> Sudden severe pain can signal an ectopic pregnancy in early pregnancy, or a problem with the placenta later on. Shoulder-tip pain alongside tummy pain is a specific warning sign of internal bleeding.

### `swelling` — I have sudden swelling

**Routes to:** maternity-unit  ·  **Evidence type:** UK guidance

**ACTION LINE (shown first, largest):**

> Call your maternity unit now if swelling comes on suddenly, or with a headache or vision changes.

**Why it matters (shown after the action):**

> Gradual swelling of ankles and feet is normal in pregnancy. A sudden increase — particularly in your face or hands, or alongside a headache — is one of the signs of pre-eclampsia.

**Reassurance (shown last, only where true):**

> Mild swelling that builds through the day and settles overnight is extremely common and not what this is about.

### `fluid` — Fluid is leaking

**Routes to:** maternity-unit  ·  **Evidence type:** UK guidance

**ACTION LINE (shown first, largest):**

### `itching` — My skin is itching, especially my hands and feet

**Routes to:** maternity-unit  ·  **Evidence type:** UK guidance

**ACTION LINE (shown first, largest):**

> Call your maternity unit and ask for a liver function blood test.

**Why it matters (shown after the action):**

> Itching with no rash, on your palms and soles, and worse at night, can mean a liver condition. Its full name is intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy, usually shortened to ICP. It needs monitoring and treatment. A blood test finds it — looking at your skin does not.

### `contractions` — I’m having regular painful contractions

**Routes to:** maternity-unit  ·  **Evidence type:** UK guidance

**ACTION LINE (shown first, largest):**

> Call your maternity unit now if you are under 37 weeks. At any stage, call if contractions come every 5 minutes, if one lasts more than 2 minutes, or if you have 6 or more in 10 minutes.

**Why it matters (shown after the action):**

> Before 37 weeks this may be premature labour, which is treatable and much better managed early. After 37 weeks it may simply be labour starting — but your unit would rather hear from you than not.

**Reassurance (shown last, only where true):**

> Irregular tightenings that come and go without a pattern are usually Braxton Hicks — practice contractions — which are normal and not labour.

### `chest` — I’m breathless or have chest pain

**Routes to:** emergency  ·  **Evidence type:** UK guidance

**ACTION LINE (shown first, largest):**

**Why it matters (shown after the action):**

> Sudden breathlessness, chest pain that is worse when you breathe in, or coughing up blood can signal a clot on the lung. Pregnancy raises clot risk, and this is treatable — but only quickly.
