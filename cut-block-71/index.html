Cooking method matters more than any ingredient

Bake, stew, poach, steam, boil — never char. Browning and searing is where dietary AGEs actually come from, not carbohydrate. The standing fear about carbs and AGEs is pointed at the wrong target and has been costing carbs. Oats in milk is close to the lowest-AGE meal it is possible to eat.

Drinks

  • Filtered water as default, especially around sessions.
  • Squash is fine with a meal, in one go. Enamel damage comes from sipping across hours, not from the drink.
  • No fizzy within 2 hours of running — gastric distension, stitch.
  • Check the squash is no-added-sugar. Regular is 10–20 kcal a glass; four or five glasses is 50–100 kcal not being counted.
  • Salt stays where it is — once a day. The lever is potassium, not sodium.

Why this is the top lever, honestly

Not "it helps recovery". At the same calorie deficit, short sleep changes what you lose — more of it comes off as muscle, less as fat. The studies are small; say so. The direction replicates.

  • Ghrelin up, leptin down — holding 1,799 gets genuinely harder.
  • Cortisol stays elevated — that is the under-eye puffiness.
  • Hard sessions land worse, which costs the times. Sleep extension has been shown to improve sprint times; the best-known study put basketball players at ten hours in bed and their sprints, reaction time and accuracy all improved. Small study — and the most directly relevant finding there is to a 400m target.
  • Skin: overnight is when barrier repair and collagen synthesis run hardest and skin blood flow is highest. Chronically raised cortisol breaks collagen down.

The phone

Most of the gain is not blue light, it is time. Moving the phone out of the room typically buys 30–60 minutes of actual sleep, faster sleep onset, and no waking to check it. The first ninety minutes of the night are the deepest, so a later bedtime cuts into the most valuable part, not the least. The rule that works is charge it in another room and use a separate alarm — not "don't look at it". Screens off 60–90 minutes before bed.

Why study is in a cut app at all

SK297 and SK298 — two 30-credit modules, 8–10 hours each, so a 16–20 hour week. Term starts Monday 28 September.

The point is not to manage the degree. It is that training load and study load draw down the same recovery, and an app that can only see the running will tell you to push in a week you are already buried.

When both are high in the same week the instruction has a direction: drop the third effort session, not the study hours. The deadline does not move and the session does.

What products cannot do

They move barrier and tone. They do not move sleep, being too lean, or how much water you are holding — and those three decide more about how your face looks on 28 November than every bottle combined.

Persistent redness across nose and cheeks is a GP or pharmacist conversation — azelaic acid 10–15% is the ingredient to raise by name, but it is not a self-start.

Facial puffiness, ranked for you

1SleepBigger than everything else combined. Short sleep raises cortisol, cortisol holds sodium, the face shows it first
2Cortisol from the deficitSustained deficit plus low carbs raises cortisol, which has genuine mineralocorticoid activity. This is why the scale flatlines then drops all at once — and an argument for not dropping carbs below the floor
3SaltYou have this backwards. Acutely yes; chronically no — kidneys adapt within days and a year on high salt has built no backlog. Keep the once-a-day salt. The lever is potassium
4GlutenSpecific to you. Accidental exposure causes inflammation and fluid retention. Puffy with no explanation — look back 24–48 hours
5Training loadHard sessions + deficit + 12,800 steps is a lot of total stress
6Sleeping positionOn your back, head slightly elevated
7AlcoholWorst single-night input there is
8DehydrationCounterintuitive — under-drinking makes you hold more

Morning puffiness is normal and resolves in 1–2 hours. Judging your face at 7 am is judging the worst version of it that exists, every day.
